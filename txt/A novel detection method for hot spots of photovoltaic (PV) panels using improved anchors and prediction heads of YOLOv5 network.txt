A novel detection method for hot spots of photovoltaic (PV) panels
using improved anchors and prediction heads of YOLOv5 network
Tianyi Suna, Huishuang Xinga, Shengxian Caoa,∗, Yanhui Zhangb, Siyuan Fana, Peng Liua

Abstract
Accurate classification and detection of hot spots of photovoltaic (PV) panels can help guide operation and maintenance
decisions, improve the power generation efficiency of the PV system, and ensure power stations’ safe and stable operation.
Considering that, in this paper, the hot spots of PV panels collected on site are taken as the research object, and their formation
mechanism is studied. Based on this, the morphological characteristics possessed by the hot spots of PV panels are classified
into circular, linear, and array ones. A novel method for detecting hot spots of PV panels based on improved anchors and
prediction heads of the YOLOv5 (AP-YOLOv5) network is proposed. Besides, to improve the detection precision of the
YOLOv5 network at different scales in hot spots of PV panels, the K-means clustering algorithm is employed to cluster the
length–width ratio of the data annotation frame, and a group of the anchors with smaller values is added so as to realize the
detection of small targets by optimizing the cluster number. Apart from that, the corresponding prediction heads are constructed
for the new anchor parameters to improve the detection precision concerning hot spots of PV panels. Furthermore, the model
is verified by training experimental data and comparing test set results. The results showed that compared with other one-stage
object detection models, the mean average precision (mAP) of the proposed network can achieve 87.8%, while the average
recall rate is 89.0%, and the F1 score reaches 88.9%. In addition, the precision of this model is better than that of other models
while maintaining a high frame rate; the frames per second (FPS) is as high as 98.6, thus laying a foundation for developing
rapid detection tool of hot spots of PV panels, improving the safety of power station operation, and providing a method for
intelligent operation and maintenance of PV power stations.

Individuals have been trying to develop a detection system for hot spots of PV panels. Chiou et al. [10] pointed

the infrared image, established the near-infrared (NIR) imaging system to capture images of the internal cracks,

mechanisms are discussed. However, there is no automatic identification, detection, and classification of hot spots of
PV panels. Aghaei et al. [12] proposed a new method of planning and drawing PV power stations using digital image
processing technology. Adopting the thermal imaging camera light unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) system for aerial
photography generates a lot of images after image stitching to describe the semantic image information, enrich and
develop an image matching algorithm of digital image processing for analysis, implementation of PV components
in the component list, and calculation about the total number of defects and health components; however, there is
no fine classification and complete discussion about hot spots of PV panels.
It is noteworthy that early target recognition and detection are mainly based on traditional machine vision
methods, while with the rapid development of modern computer science, the convolutional neural network (CNN)
has more advantages in dealing with multi-object image detection problems with complex environmental states and
variable detection parameters [13,14]. At present, the CNN has been widely used in image target recognition and
detection in large-scale production environments, such as transportation, industry, agriculture, and breeding [15].
Yang et al. [16] put forward a crack detection transfer learning method based on the dynamic convolution neural
network (DCNN) and applied it to crack detection of bridges, highways, and other infrastructure. [17] adopted
image-based algorithms to realize image fire detection. Therefore, it can be seen that due to the superiority of the
convolutional neural network in multi-object image detection, it gradually becomes a popular detection method in
various fields.
This study aims to develop a more accurate method of detecting the hot spots in PV panels. A novel method
for detecting the hot spots of PV panels based on improved anchors and prediction heads of the YOLOv5 (AP-
YOLOv5) network is proposed in this work. This model is a detection method for hot spots of PV panels based
on the latest generation of the one-stage object detection YOLOv5 network, which is improved to achieve rapid
detection and accurate classification of hot spots in PV panels. In order to provide a reliable basis for the operation
and maintenance of PV power stations, in this study, the hot spots of PV panels are accurately classified, and their
formation mechanism is also researched. In this case, the classification concerning the hot spots of PV panels is
achieved. Apart from that, for the purpose of accurately identifying the small size hot spots of PV panels, the
K-means method is used to cluster the length to width ratio of the labeling frame belonging to the hot spots of
PV panels in this paper, so as to conduct optimized anchor research, further improve the network structure of the
YOLOv5 network, enhance the prediction heads, and realize the identification and detection of the hot spots in PV

2. Problem description
2.1. Formation mechanism of hot spots in PV panels
The hot spots are prevalent in PV panels in operation. In order to provide theoretical support for PV operation
and maintenance, this study first researched the formation mechanism of hot spots of PV panels and provided a
theoretical basis for the classification of hot spots in PV panels. The formation process of hot spots in PV panels is
shown in Fig. 1. After the PV panels are partially blocked, they cannot absorb light energy normally, thus blocking
the photoelectric effect of silicon semiconductors. Through the power consumption parts (internal resistance), the
current will generate heat, and the battery, after a long time shielding, completes the accumulation of heat to form
hot spots of PV panels.
Fig. 1. Formation process of hot spots of PV panels.
Therefore, it can be analyzed that once the PV cell unit generates hot spot defects in the working state, the fault
area will appear in a short period, which will eventually lead to the burning of the cell and the stopping of the
power supply of the whole battery string, affecting the safe operation of the PV module, and even triggering a fire.
2.2. Classification of hot spots in PV panels
Through the research on the formation mechanism of hot spots of PV panels, it can be found that hot spots of
PV panels are usually formed due to local occlusion, and the operation process of PV panels is affected by the
natural environment and components themselves. By inductive analysis, hot spots of PV panels can be divided into
three classes in shape: round, linear, and square ones, which can represent various hot spots of PV panels common
in the field operation of PV power stations. Fig. 2 shows the three typical types of hot spots in PV panels. To
improve the pertinence of hot spot detection in the PV panels, according to their characteristics formed by different
fault types, the hot spots of PV panels are classified into round, linear, and square forms. Fig. 2(a), (b), and (c)
respectively display the infrared images of PV panels with three types of hot spots obtained by thermal-imaging
equipment. The study on the types of hot spots in PV panels and their causes will help diagnose the failure types of
PV panels accurately and effectively, thus implementing further operation and maintenance measures. By analyzing

3. Methodology
3.1. AP-YOLOv5 network

YOLOv4 network, and consists of several important panels, such as Focus, CSP, SPPNet, Panet, and prediction
heads, which constitute the network backbone structure. This paper optimizes the profile of small targets for the
YOLOv5 network target detection framework. Modify the initial anchors, increase the prediction heads, and improve
the network’s ability to detect small target objects by constructing large-scale feature images. The contributions of

3.1.2. Anchor optimization
Anchors are the benchmark that the algorithm uses to predict the bounding box, and choosing the priori box
with the most appropriate size will reduce the difficulty of training. There is an obvious difference between the
shape of the test object and the initial value in the PV panel hot spot data, and small-scale dense objects such as
bird droppings and fallen leaves also exist. Besides, it is not easy to achieve satisfactory precision by directly using
the initial anchor value. Here, it should be mentioned that, this study tended to find anchor parameters suitable for
the PV panel hot spot detection task, and then use a feature map with more information about the tiny target to
detect the small target. By employing K-means clustering data, a length–width ratio of frame annotation, and by
modifying the cluster number, a group of anchors with the small value are added for meeting the detection of small
targets. Moreover, the following four groups of 12 prior frames were obtained according to the characteristics of
the detected category object, as Fig. 4 shown, and they were (18*23), (28*19), (13*39), (22*30), (37*31), (14*95),
(68*25), (59*43), (23*117), (86*37), (22*220), and (198*45). In addition, using the K-means clustering method to

3.1.3. Dding the prediction heads
Each set of anchors represents a different feature diagram. The top part of Fig. 4 is the initialized YOLOv5
network with the three sets of initialized anchors and their corresponding schematic, and the bottom part is the four
sets of clustered AP-YOLOv5 network anchors. When the input image size is 640*640, the first line is the anchor
frame on the added maximum feature graph, and the corresponding detection layer size is 160*160, which can be
used to detect small target objects (4*4). Besides, this layer possesses the unique structure of the AP-YOLOv5
network. The anchor frame on the feature map in the middle corresponding to the second and third lines has a
detection layer size of 80*80 and 40*40, respectively, which can be adopted to detect medium-sized targets with
a size above 8*8 and 16*16 scales. Furthermore, the fourth line is the anchor frame on the smallest feature map,
and the corresponding detection layer size is 20*20, which can be employed to detect large targets above 32*32.
There are only three sets of anchors initialized, and the corresponding YOLOv5 network has only three prediction
heads. After clustering optimization, a set of small target anchors has been added, and accordingly, a small target
detection layer needs to be added to the prediction heads. As shown in Fig. 3, after the network enters the sampling
layer for the first time, the feature graph continues to be up-sampled, making the feature graph continue to expand.
At the same time, at the second CSP1 3 layer, the feature map of 160*160 obtained from up-sampling is concat
fused with the feature map of the backbone network to obtain a larger feature map for small target detection. Finally,
a small target detection layer of 160*160*255 is added to the prediction heads. Moreover, the modified network
uses four prediction heads for detection, with dimensions corresponding to 160*160*255, 80*80*255, 40*40*255,
and 20*20*255, respectively. The small target prediction heads with 160*160 prediction heads are more suitable
for the actual demand of PV component hot spot detection, which is conducive to identifying different types of hot
spots of PV panels accurately.

4. Results
In this study, to achieve high-precision real-time detection of the hot spots in PV panels, anchors were optimized
in the YOLOv5 network backbone, and the corresponding President layer was constructed. Besides, customized
image data sets were used for training and testing, while the prediction performance of the YOLOv5 network, SSD
model, and optimized AP-YOLOv5 network was evaluated by training the YOLOv5 model and SSD model as the
control group.
4.1. Data preparation
An image dataset was established in this study to train and verify the AP-YOLOv5 network. There are three
classifications in this data set: round, linear, and square ones. These images were collected from the site of the
PV power station and the setup test stand. In order to avoid over-fitting in training, image enhancement such as
inversion, contrast, rotation shift, and CutMix was carried out on the data set.
To train the AP-YOLOv5 network, the image data first should be manually labeled. Then, the labeled images can
be used as ground truth and can be compared with the predicted results generated by the training model to evaluate
the precision of training. Other than that, using the popular annotation tool Labelme [19], a total of 1000 images
have been tagged. Afterwards, the dataset was randomly divided into two groups, one group using 800 images for
training and the other group using 200 images for verification.
4.2. Training and validation
The software and hardware tools involved in model training and test experiments in this paper are as follows:
CPU — Xeon E5-2630, GPU — NVIDIA Quadro2000, Memory — 64 GB, System — Windows Server 2012r2,
Development environment torch1.6.0.According to server memory and graphics card configuration, the experimental
parameter setting method is as follows: the small-batch gradient descent method is iterated 500 times, with the
momentum set to 0.9, learning rate to 0.01, weight attenuation rate to 0.001, and batch size to 8. Besides, other
parameters are the original parameters of the YOLOv5 model.
4.3. Comparison with other models
The SSD network is introduced for comparing the algorithm model better suitable for detecting the hot spots of
PV panels. Like the YOLOv5 network, the SSD network belongs to the one-stage object detection model, which can
directly generate the object’s category probability and position coordinate value. The final detection result can be
directly obtained through a single detection. Besides, in Fig. 5, precision(a), recall(b), mAP@.5(c), mAP@0.95(d),
and loss(e) of the YOLOv5 network, SSD network, and AP-YOLOv5 network are compared.
Precision is the ratio of positive network predictions to positive network predictions, and Recall is the ratio of
positive network predictions to positive network predictions. It can be seen from Fig. 5(a) and (b) that the AP-
YOLOv5 network witnesses a great improvement in recall rate and precision rate, especially in the detection of
small linear targets, where the precision and recall rate have increased by 0.34 and 0.224, accordingly.

4.4. Comprehensive evaluation
Based on the above experimental results, it can be seen that the AP-YOLOv5 network performs better than the
YOLOv5 network, and the average precision of recognition, average recall rate, and other performance indicators
have increased dramatically, especially for the recognition task with small targets. Therefore, it is correct and
reasonable to adopt the anchor prior frame adapting to hot spot samples and enhance network detection layers
to improve the detection ability of small network targets. Therefore, it can be concluded that the AP-YOLOv5
network has the best comprehensive performance in the PV panel hot spot detection task, and its detection results
are 87.8% average accuracy, 89.0% average recall rate, 88.9% F1 score and 98.6 FPS, which meet the requirements
of the hot spot detection.

5. Conclusions and future work
In this study, a real-time detection framework for the PV panel hot spot type is proposed. Studying the mechanism
concerning the hot spots of PV panels aims to help PV power stations improve the efficiency and precision of their
detection, provide a basis for operation and maintenance decision making, and ensure the safe and stable operation of
power stations. The proposed detection model is based on the YOLOv5 network, the recently published one-stage
object detection network. The K-means method is also adopted to cluster the length–width ratio of the standard
frame of data, optimize the original anchor parameters of the backbone network, expand the small target detection
anchors, and add the Prediction Head layer to the backbone network. A total of 1000 images, including circular,
linear, and array hot spots of PV panels, were used for training and verification. Besides, the improved model and
the original YOLOv5 network were trained and evaluated on various scales based on Precision, Recall, and mAP.
Furthermore, to verify the validity of the model, the same series of tests were compared with the YOLOv5 network,
YOLOv4 network, and YOLOv3 network, and the performance of different series was compared with that of the
Faster R-CNN network and SSD network. The experimental results show that the AP-YOLOv5 network proposed
in this study is superior to other models in mAP (87.8%), average recall rate (89.0%), and F1 score (88.9%) while
ensuring a high frame rate (98.6). Apart from that, better detection performance in field practice is demonstrated,
and the experimental results reveal that the AP-YOLOv5 network is capable of detecting the hot spots of PV panels.
This is the first attempt of the improved YOLOv5 network in the classification and detection of the hot spots in
PV panels. This study demonstrates the possibility of applying cutting-edge deep learning techniques to the field
application of PV power plants. However, there is still room for improvement in PV panel hot spot detection, such as
frame rate. Considering that, future work will optimize detection speed and accuracy, reduce computational power
requirements, and provide more possibilities for portable field detection.
